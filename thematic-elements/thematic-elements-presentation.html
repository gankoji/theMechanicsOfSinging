1. Introduction

Goal: Every musical choice connects to one of four themes: Melody, Lyric, Harmony, Rhythm.
Think of these as the pillars of expressive singing and interpretation.

2. Thematic Deep Dives

Melody

What is melody? Melody is a linear succession of musical tones perceived as a single entity. It combines pitch and rhythm, and is often the most memorable part of a song. Melodies give music its identity and emotional shape.

  • Draw phrase shapes/contours to visualize the rise and fall of the tune.
  • Plan breath placement at peaks and turnarounds for phrasing.
  • Listen for motifs and repetition; these help anchor the melody in memory.
Actionable Tips:
  • Mark phrase shapes in your score.
  • Practice singing with varied dynamics to shape the melody.
  • Try singing the opening phrase of “O Canada” and notice its contour.
Demo: Call & response: Sing the opening phrase of “O Canada” together, focusing on phrase shape.

Lyric

Why are lyrics important? Lyrics tell the story and drive the emotional content of a song. Diction and word emphasis shape how the message is received.

  • Use diction choices for nuance: experiment with vowel shapes and consonant clarity.
  • Emphasize key words to highlight the story.
  • Vary your delivery to match the song’s mood and meaning.
Actionable Tips:
  • Use a vowel emphasis chart to practice clarity.
  • Try speaking or singing a simple poem, varying diction for interpretation.
  • Mark lyric keywords in your score for emphasis.
Exercise: Speak or sing a short poem, experimenting with diction and emphasis for different interpretations.

Harmony

What is harmony? Harmony is the combination of different musical notes played or sung simultaneously to produce chords and chord progressions. It adds depth, color, and emotional tension or release to music.

  • Listen to your “neighbor” part and match vowel and timbre for blend.
  • Tune 3rds and 5ths carefully; use a drone for sectional tuning.
  • Understand how chords move and resolve for expressive phrasing.
Actionable Tips:
  • Practice singing over a drone (I–IV–V–I) to tune intervals.
  • Blend with your section by matching vowel shapes and dynamics.
  • Notice how tension and release in harmony affect the mood.
Demo: Director drones I–IV–V–I; singers blend and tune their parts.
Learn more about Harmony →

Rhythm

What is rhythm? Rhythm is the pattern of sounds and silences in music, creating pulse, groove, and movement. It includes beat, tempo, and syncopation.

  • Mark strong beats and ties in your music.
  • Practice subdivision tapping before singing.
  • Experiment with rubato (flexible timing) for expressive phrasing.
Actionable Tips:
  • Clap and sing a syncopated barbershop tag together.
  • Use a metronome or tap your foot to internalize the pulse.
  • Try holding ties and marking subdivisions in your score.
Demo: Clap and sing a syncopated tag as a group.

3. Recap & Q&A

Key Takeaways:
  • Every musical choice can be traced to melody, lyric, harmony, or rhythm.
  • Expressive singing comes from understanding and balancing these elements.
  • Practice actionable tips for each theme to improve as a musician.

5. Ongoing Suggestions

  • Weekly warm-ups: Vowel-matching on chords, rhythm clapping patterns
  • Repertoire annotations: Mark melody jumps & lyric keywords in scores
  • Minute drills: Quick echo-phrases (melody), one-word emphasis (lyrics)
  • Recording & feedback: Assign one member per theme to note observations each week
